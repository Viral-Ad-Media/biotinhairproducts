Biotin shampoos are easy to find, which also makes them a target for counterfeits and old stock. Where you buy matters almost as much as what you buy.

## Pharmacies and drugstores

Large pharmacy chains carry the big drugstore names such as OGX and Renpure. Stock turns over quickly, so you're unlikely to get an old bottle, and returns are simple.

## Supermarkets and big-box stores

Supermarkets and general retailers usually carry a smaller range at good prices. They're a sensible choice for familiar brands you've used before.

## Beauty retailers

Specialist beauty stores stock salon and premium lines such as Briogeo and often let you try samples. Staff can be helpful if you're unsure which formula suits your hair type.

## Brand websites

Buying direct from the brand guarantees the product is genuine and fresh. Brands often run subscriptions and bundle deals on shampoo and conditioner sets.

## Online marketplaces

Marketplaces like Amazon and Walmart are convenient and often cheapest, but they're also where most fakes are sold. To stay safe:

- Buy from the brand's official store or from listings "shipped and sold by" the marketplace itself.
- Check reviews for complaints about strange smells, watery texture or different packaging.
- Be wary of prices far below everywhere else.
- Check the batch code and expiry date when the bottle arrives.

## Signs of a fake or bad bottle

- Misspellings or blurry printing on the label
- A seal that's missing or already broken
- A smell, color or texture that's different from what you remember
- No batch number or expiry date

If something feels off, don't use it. Contact the seller and the brand.

## The bottom line

Pharmacies, beauty retailers and brand sites are the safest places to buy. If you shop on a marketplace, stick to official stores and check the bottle when it arrives.
