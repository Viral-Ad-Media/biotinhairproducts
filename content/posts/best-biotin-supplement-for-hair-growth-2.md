Biotin supplements are sold everywhere with big claims about hair growth. Here's how to tell whether you need one and how to pick a good one.

> This article is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before starting a supplement, especially if you're pregnant, take other medicines or have upcoming blood tests.

## Who benefits from biotin

Biotin helps hair most in people who are low in it. That can include people with certain genetic conditions, long-term use of some anti-seizure medicines, heavy alcohol use, or very restrictive diets. For most others, extra biotin hasn't been shown to boost hair growth.

## How much is enough

The adequate intake for adults is about 30 micrograms (mcg) a day. Many hair supplements contain 5,000 to 10,000 mcg, hundreds of times more. Biotin is water-soluble and generally considered low risk, but there's no evidence that mega-doses work better.

## Important: biotin and blood tests

High-dose biotin can interfere with some lab tests, including thyroid and troponin tests used to check for heart attacks, producing falsely high or low results. The US FDA has warned about this. Tell your doctor you take biotin, and ask whether to pause it before blood work.

## What to look for

### Third-party testing

Supplements aren't tested by regulators before sale. Look for a seal from an independent tester such as USP, NSF or Informed Choice, which confirms the label matches what's inside.

### A sensible dose

A lower dose is often all you need. Higher isn't better.

### Simple ingredients

Avoid long "proprietary blends" that don't list amounts. If you want a multi-nutrient hair formula, check that it doesn't double up on vitamin A or selenium, which can cause harm (and even hair loss) in excess.

### The right form

Capsules and tablets are usually cheapest and sugar-free. Gummies are easy to take but contain sugar and can be less precise. See our comparison of [biotin gummies vs pills](/biotin-gummies-vs-biotin-pills).

## Alternatives to consider

If your hair is thinning, ask your doctor to check iron (ferritin), vitamin D, thyroid function and zinc. Correcting a real deficiency usually does far more than adding biotin.

## The bottom line

The best biotin supplement is a third-party tested product with a modest dose, taken because you have a reason to. For most people, a balanced diet and a check-up are the better first step.
