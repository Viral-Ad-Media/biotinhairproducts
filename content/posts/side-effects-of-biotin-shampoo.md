Biotin itself is very well tolerated, and the small amount in a shampoo that gets rinsed away is unlikely to cause problems. When people react to a biotin shampoo, it's almost always to something else in the bottle.

![Woman thinking about her hair](/uploads/2022/02/lady-thinking.jpg)

## Common reactions

### Itching, redness or flaking

Fragrance, essential oils and some preservatives are the most common causes of scalp irritation. If your scalp itches or turns red after switching shampoos, stop using it and see whether the symptoms settle.

### Dryness

Strong cleansers such as sodium lauryl sulfate can strip natural oils, leaving the scalp tight and the ends dry. Curly, coily and color-treated hair is most prone to this. Look for "sulfate-free" or milder cleansers if this happens.

### Build-up and limp hair

Some thickening shampoos leave proteins or polymers on the hair. Used every day without a clarifying wash, they can make hair feel stiff or weighed down.

### Allergic reactions

Rarely, people are allergic to an ingredient such as a botanical extract, a preservative or wheat protein. Swelling, hives or a burning feeling are reasons to rinse right away and seek medical advice.

## Do a patch test

Before using a new shampoo on your whole head, put a small amount behind your ear or on your inner elbow, leave it for a few minutes, rinse, and check the spot over the next 24 to 48 hours.

## What about biotin supplements?

Supplements are a different story from shampoo. High-dose biotin pills and gummies can interfere with some blood tests, including thyroid and heart (troponin) tests, and give falsely high or low results. If you take biotin, tell your doctor before any blood work. Some people also report breakouts on high doses.

## When to stop

Stop using a product and talk to a doctor or pharmacist if you get:

- Persistent itching, burning or redness
- Sudden increased shedding after starting the product
- Any swelling or hives

## The bottom line

For most people, biotin shampoo has no side effects beyond what any shampoo might cause. Choose a formula that suits your scalp, patch test first, and you're very unlikely to run into trouble.
