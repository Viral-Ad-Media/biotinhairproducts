It depends on what you mean by "work." Biotin shampoo can make hair look and feel thicker. There's little good evidence that it makes more hair grow.

## What the science says

Biotin (vitamin B7) helps your body make keratin. Taking biotin by mouth clearly helps people who are deficient, and their hair, skin and nails often improve. But deficiency is uncommon, and studies haven't shown that extra biotin helps people who already get enough.

For shampoos, the evidence is even thinner. Shampoo spends a minute or two on your scalp before it's rinsed away, and there's no strong research showing biotin is absorbed through the scalp in amounts that change hair growth.

## Why people still see results

Users often report fuller, stronger-looking hair, and that's genuine. It usually comes from the rest of the formula:

- **Volumizing cleansers and polymers** lift hair at the root.
- **Proteins** like keratin and collagen coat and strengthen strands.
- **Less breakage** means you keep more length and density over time.
- **A cleaner scalp** removes build-up that can make hair look flat.

## What to expect, week by week

- **First wash:** more volume and a lighter feel, especially on fine hair.
- **Weeks 2 to 4:** smoother hair, fewer broken strands in your brush.
- **Weeks 6 to 12:** if breakage was your main problem, hair may look longer and denser.

If you're shedding more than usual, a shampoo is unlikely to fix it. Shedding has many causes, and a doctor can help find yours.

## Who it's best for

- People with fine, flat or breakage-prone hair who want more body
- Anyone looking for a gentle daily shampoo with added proteins
- People pairing it with medical treatment who want cosmetic support

## The bottom line

Biotin shampoo works as a cosmetic product, not a hair-loss treatment. If you want fuller-looking hair, it's worth a try. If you're losing hair, see a professional.
