Short answer: biotin can help regrow hair in people whose hair loss is caused by a biotin deficiency. For everyone else, there's no good evidence it regrows hair.

## Why biotin got its reputation

Severe biotin deficiency causes hair thinning, a scaly rash and brittle nails. When deficient people take biotin, those symptoms improve, sometimes dramatically. That observation turned into a broad belief that biotin helps everyone's hair.

## What studies show

Reviews of the research have found that the cases where biotin improved hair growth involved people with an underlying deficiency or a specific medical condition. Studies in healthy people with normal biotin levels haven't shown a meaningful benefit.

## Who might be low in biotin

- People with biotinidase deficiency, a rare genetic condition
- Long-term users of certain anti-seizure medicines
- People with heavy alcohol use or some digestive disorders
- People who regularly eat large amounts of raw egg whites
- Some pregnant people, whose needs increase

If any of these apply, ask your doctor whether testing or supplementation makes sense.

## Common causes of hair loss that biotin won't fix

- **Pattern hair loss** (androgenetic alopecia), the most common type in both men and women
- **Telogen effluvium**, shedding after stress, illness, surgery, childbirth or crash dieting
- **Iron deficiency and thyroid problems**
- **Alopecia areata**, an autoimmune condition causing patchy loss
- **Traction alopecia** from tight hairstyles

Each has its own treatment, from minoxidil to correcting iron levels to changing hairstyles, and a doctor or dermatologist can help.

## A note on blood tests

High-dose biotin can distort some lab results, including thyroid and heart tests. If you take it, tell your doctor before blood work.

## The bottom line

Biotin isn't a hair-regrowth treatment for most people. If your hair is thinning, find out why first. You'll get far better results treating the actual cause.
