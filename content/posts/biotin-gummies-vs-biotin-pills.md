Biotin comes in two main forms: chewable gummies and swallowable pills (tablets or capsules). Both deliver the same vitamin. The differences are in everything around it.

## Quick comparison

| | Gummies | Pills |
|---|---|---|
| Taste | Pleasant, fruity | None |
| Sugar | Usually 2 to 4 g per serving | Usually none |
| Dose accuracy | Can vary more over shelf life | Typically more consistent |
| Extra ingredients | Gelatin or pectin, flavors, colors | Fillers, capsule shell |
| Cost per dose | Higher | Lower |
| Easy to swallow | Yes | Depends on size |

## Absorption

Biotin is well absorbed in both forms. Neither is clearly "better absorbed" in a way that matters for hair.

## Dose accuracy

Gummies are harder to manufacture precisely, and vitamins can break down faster in them. Independent testing has found some gummies don't contain what the label says. Pills tend to be more consistent. Either way, choose a product with third-party testing (such as USP or NSF).

## Sugar and teeth

Gummies usually contain added sugar and can stick to teeth. If you're watching sugar, have diabetes, or take several gummy vitamins a day, pills are the better choice. Sugar-free gummies exist but often use sugar alcohols that can upset some stomachs.

## Safety

Gummies look and taste like candy, so keep them away from children. And whichever form you take, high-dose biotin can interfere with some blood tests. Tell your doctor you take it.

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose pills** if you want the lowest cost, no sugar and the most consistent dose.
- **Choose gummies** if you hate swallowing pills and would otherwise skip them.

The form matters less than taking a quality, tested product for a good reason. If you're unsure whether you need biotin at all, read [does biotin regrow hair?](/does-biotin-regrow-hair) first.
