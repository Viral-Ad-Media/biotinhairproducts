Most biotin shampoos promise "thicker, fuller hair." The label on the back tells you far more than the front. Use this checklist before you buy.

## 1. Match the formula to your hair type

- **Fine or thin hair:** lightweight, volumizing formulas without heavy oils or butters.
- **Dry, curly or coily hair:** moisturizing, sulfate-free formulas with oils or butters.
- **Oily scalp:** a clarifying formula, or alternate your biotin shampoo with one.
- **Color-treated hair:** sulfate-free and labeled color-safe.

## 2. Check the cleanser

Sulfates such as sodium lauryl sulfate clean well but can be drying. Gentler options include sodium cocoyl isethionate, cocamidopropyl betaine and decyl glucoside.

## 3. Look for helpful supporting ingredients

Biotin is rarely the star. These ingredients often do more of the visible work:

- **Hydrolyzed proteins** (keratin, collagen, wheat or rice) to strengthen and add body
- **Panthenol** (pro-vitamin B5) for moisture and shine
- **Niacinamide or caffeine** for scalp care
- **Aloe, argan or coconut oil** for softness

## 4. Watch for things that may not suit you

- Strong fragrance or many essential oils if your scalp is sensitive
- Heavy silicones if your hair goes flat easily
- Wheat protein if you have a known sensitivity

## 5. Be realistic about claims

Be cautious of products promising to "regrow" hair or to stop hair loss. Shampoo stays on your scalp for a minute or two; it can improve how your hair looks and feels, but it can't treat medical hair loss.

## 6. Buy from a trusted seller

Fakes are common online. Buy from pharmacies, beauty retailers, the brand's own website, or official brand stores on marketplaces. See our guide on [where to buy biotin shampoo](/where-to-buy-biotin-shampoo-for-hair).

## The bottom line

The best biotin shampoo is the one that suits your hair type and scalp. Read the ingredients, skip the miracle claims, and give it a month before you judge it.
